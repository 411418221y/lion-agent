## Summary
Most majors have at least one foundational course that unlocks later courses. This document explains the concept and how to find it for ANY major.

## What is a 'gateway course'
A gateway (foundational) course is an early required course that:
- is listed near the beginning of a major sequence
- is a prerequisite for multiple later courses
- reduces planning risk if taken early

## Step-by-step: find the gateway course for ANY major
1) Go to majors index:
   https://bulletin.columbia.edu/general-studies/majors-concentrations/
2) Open the user’s major page
3) Look for the earliest required sequence:
   - "Intro to ___"
   - "Foundations of ___"
   - "Prerequisite for upper-level courses"
4) Confirm by checking at least 2 later courses in the major:
   - Do they list this course (or its sequence) as prerequisite?
5) Recommend it as the first-semester anchor

## Example (CS only — example, not universal)
- Example idea: many students treat an intro programming course as a gateway because later CS courses have prerequisites.
- If user is CS and asks “what should I take first”, you can mention:
  "COMS W1004 is commonly used as an intro gateway (confirm via the CS/major pages)."
- Always also provide the CS major page link:
  https://bulletin.columbia.edu/general-studies/majors-concentrations/computer-science/#text

## Safety rule
Do NOT claim a specific course is required for a major unless the major page explicitly says so.
When unsure, say: "Confirm on the official major page."
